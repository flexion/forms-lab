# Pardon Application — Policy Excerpts

Three verbatim excerpts from the Department of Justice regulations
governing presidential clemency petitions. These regulations are
printed on the application form itself and cited in the Pardon
Attorney's instructions to applicants.

## Section — 28 CFR 1.1 (Submission of petition; form to be used; contents of petition)

Petitions for pardon, commutation of sentence, remission of fine or
restitution, or reprieve shall be addressed to the President of the
United States and shall be submitted to the Pardon Attorney,
Department of Justice, Washington, DC 20530, except for petitions
relating to military offenses. The petitioner shall use the form
prescribed by the Attorney General, furnish the information called for
therein, and swear to the truth of the application before a notary
public or other person authorized to administer oaths. Petitions and
other required forms may be obtained from the Pardon Attorney. A
petition signed in blank, or without answers to the questions
contained in the prescribed form, or without the required signed
statements of the character witnesses, will not be accepted for
filing.

## Section — 28 CFR 1.2 (Eligibility for filing petition for pardon)

No petition for pardon should be filed until the expiration of a
waiting period of at least five years after the date of the release of
the petitioner from confinement or, in case no prison sentence was
imposed, until the expiration of a period of at least five years after
the date of the conviction of the petitioner. Generally, no petition
should be submitted by a person who is on probation, parole, or
supervised release. In the case of a petitioner convicted of a
violation of the Uniform Code of Military Justice, the waiting period
shall be computed from the date of the petitioner's release from
confinement or from the date of the imposition of sentence if no
sentence of confinement was imposed. The Attorney General may in his
or her discretion shorten the waiting period in cases involving
unusual circumstances.

## Section — 28 CFR 1.6 (Consideration of petitions; disclosure of files)

Upon receipt of a petition for executive clemency, the Attorney
General shall cause such investigation to be made of the matter as he
or she may deem necessary and appropriate, using the services of, or
obtaining reports from, appropriate officials and agencies of the
Government, including the Federal Bureau of Investigation. In
clemency cases involving offenses against the United States prosecuted
by a United States Attorney, the Attorney General shall request the
United States Attorney for the district of conviction to provide
comments and recommendations on the clemency application. A petition
for pardon shall include a statement of the specific purpose for which
pardon is sought and, if applicable, copies of any relevant documents
that support the petition. The petitioner shall also submit affidavits
from three responsible persons who have known the petitioner for at
least three years, attesting to the petitioner's good character,
reputation, and, where relevant, employment history. Upon a showing of
exceptional circumstances, the Attorney General may waive any of the
procedural requirements of this subpart.
