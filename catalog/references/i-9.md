# I-9 — Policy Excerpts

Three verbatim excerpts from the employment eligibility verification
regulations. The I-9 form is the documentary implementation of
8 CFR 274a.2; field sensitivity and document requirements flow
directly from this regulatory text.

## Section — 8 CFR 274a.2(b)(1)(i) (Employer attestation and document examination)

Before the end of the employee's first day of employment, the employee
shall complete Section 1 of Form I-9 by providing information relating
to his or her identity and attesting, under penalty of perjury, that
he or she is a citizen or national of the United States, a lawful
permanent resident, or an alien authorized to work in the United
States. The employee shall also provide his or her date of birth, and
may voluntarily provide a U.S. Social Security account number, an
email address, and a telephone number. Within three business days of
the hire, or in the case of an individual hired for a period of less
than three business days, at the time the employment commences, the
employer shall physically examine the original document or documents
presented by the employee and complete Section 2 of Form I-9,
recording the document title, issuing authority, document number, and
expiration date (if any), as shown on the document or documents. The
employer shall sign the attestation on Form I-9, certifying, under
penalty of perjury, that the documents presented appear to be genuine
and to relate to the individual.

## Section — 8 CFR 274a.2(b)(1)(v) (List A, List B, and List C documents)

An individual may satisfy the document examination requirement of this
section by presenting to his or her employer one document from List A,
which establishes both identity and employment authorization; or one
document from List B establishing identity in combination with one
document from List C establishing employment authorization. List A
documents include a U.S. passport, a Permanent Resident Card
(Form I-551), and an Employment Authorization Document that contains a
photograph (Form I-766). List B documents include a state-issued
driver's license, a state-issued identification card, and a U.S.
military card or draft record. List C documents include a U.S. Social
Security account number card (other than one that specifies on its
face that the issuance of the card does not authorize employment in
the United States), a Certification of Birth Abroad, and a certified
copy of a birth certificate issued by a State, county, municipal
authority, or outlying possession of the United States.

## Section — 8 CFR 274a.2(b)(2) (Retention; reverification)

The employer shall retain the Form I-9 for a period of three years
after the date of hire or one year after the date employment is
terminated, whichever is later. If an employee's employment
authorization expires, the employer must reverify the employee's
employment authorization on or before the expiration date by examining
either a document that shows employment authorization has been
extended or a new document from List A or List C, and shall complete
Section 3 of Form I-9. U.S. citizens and noncitizen nationals never
need to be reverified. Lawful permanent residents who present a
Form I-551 in Section 2 do not need to be reverified.
