# Wisconsin SNAP Application — Policy Excerpts

Policy excerpts from 7 CFR 273 governing the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP). These excerpts establish household composition rules, income and resource requirements, work obligations, deduction calculations, and procedural requirements for application filing, expedited service, and certification. Wisconsin administers SNAP under these federal regulations with state-specific implementation through FoodShare.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.2(a) (Application filing and processing)

The State agency shall provide a simple application form to households and shall accept an application when it is signed by a responsible household member or the household's authorized representative and the household's name and address are provided. The application form must be filed in the project area office serving the area in which the household resides. The household may file an application on the same day it contacts the State agency in person during office hours. A household that mails an application or submits it through electronic means must be provided an opportunity to complete the application. Upon receipt of a completed application, the State agency shall determine eligibility and provide benefits no later than 30 calendar days after the date of application, except for expedited service cases as specified in paragraph (i) of this section. The State agency shall conduct an interview with a household member or authorized representative prior to certification. The interview may be conducted in person or by telephone, at the option of the State agency. If neither a household member nor an authorized representative appears for a scheduled interview, the State agency shall send a notice providing the household another opportunity to schedule an interview.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.2(i) (Expedited service screening)

Households in which all members have little or no income shall be provided expedited service. Households with monthly gross income that is less than one hundred fifty dollars and liquid resources that do not exceed one hundred dollars shall be screened for expedited service. Additionally, households with combined monthly gross income and liquid resources that are less than the household's monthly rent or mortgage and utilities shall be screened for expedited service. Migrant or seasonal farmworker households with liquid resources that do not exceed one hundred dollars who are destitute shall also be screened for expedited service. Destitute is defined as a household that has received income of less than twenty-five dollars in the month of application. The State agency shall ensure that expedited service households that are entitled to expedited service receive benefits no later than seven calendar days following the date the application was filed. The State agency shall not require verification prior to providing expedited service except verification of identity. After the household receives expedited benefits, the State agency must verify all information necessary to determine household eligibility and benefit levels prior to providing additional benefits.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.1(b) (Household composition)

A household is defined as an individual who lives alone or who, while living with others, customarily purchases food and prepares meals for home consumption separate and apart from the others, or a group of individuals who live together and customarily purchase food and prepare meals together for home consumption. Spouses and their children under twenty-two years of age who live with them are considered to be a single household, even if they purchase and prepare meals separately. Parents and their children under twenty-two years of age who live with them are considered to be a single household, even if they purchase and prepare meals separately. Elderly or disabled persons and their spouses may be a separate household if the others with whom they reside and share meals do not have gross income exceeding one hundred sixty-five percent of the poverty level. The State agency shall determine household composition according to the statements of the applicant, but may establish that applicant's statements are incorrect or incomplete through verification. The determination of household composition must be made based on the living arrangements and the food purchasing and preparation practices of the household members as they existed on the date of application, not on a prior or contemplated arrangement.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.4 (Citizenship and immigration status)

To be eligible for SNAP benefits, an individual must be either a United States citizen or a qualified alien as defined in 8 USC 1641. United States citizens include persons born in the fifty states, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, and the Northern Mariana Islands. Qualified aliens include lawful permanent residents, refugees, asylees, aliens paroled for at least one year, aliens whose deportation is being withheld, aliens granted conditional entry, certain battered aliens, victims of trafficking, and Cuban and Haitian entrants. Certain qualified aliens are subject to additional eligibility restrictions based on their date of entry and must satisfy work history requirements or be members of exempt groups. The State agency shall verify the citizenship or immigration status of all household members applying for benefits. For U.S. citizens, a signed declaration under penalty of perjury is sufficient verification. For qualified aliens, the State agency shall verify immigration status through the SAVE system maintained by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services. The State agency may not deny benefits to eligible household members based on the immigration status of ineligible household members in the same household.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.9(b) (Income: earned and unearned)

Earned income includes wages and salaries from employment, net self-employment income after allowable deductions, training allowances from vocational and rehabilitation programs, and certain vendor payments counted as income to the household. Unearned income includes but is not limited to assistance payments from other programs such as TANF, SSI, and general assistance; Social Security, veterans' benefits, and unemployment compensation; gross income from rental property, interest and dividends; and cash contributions from individuals outside the household. The State agency shall count all earned and unearned income received by all household members, except as specifically excluded by statute or regulation. Income received by ineligible household members shall be counted in its entirety to the eligible members. The State agency shall count income as of the date it is received by the household or paid to a third party on behalf of the household, not when it is earned or becomes due. The amount counted shall be the amount received before any deductions including taxes, garnishments, or voluntary deductions. For households receiving income on a recurring monthly basis, the State agency shall count the income received during the month. For households receiving income on other than a monthly basis, the State agency shall convert the income to a monthly amount.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.8 (Resources and BBCE exclusions)

Resources that are not excluded under this section shall be counted in their entirety in determining eligibility. The resource limit for most households is two thousand five hundred dollars. For households with an elderly or disabled member, the resource limit is three thousand seven hundred and fifty dollars. Excluded resources include the home and surrounding property that is not separated from the home by property owned by others; household goods and personal effects; life insurance policies and pension plans that are not accessible to the household; property essential to employment or self-employment; resources of students under eighteen years of age; earned income tax credits received in the month of receipt and the following month; and vehicles to the extent that their equity value is excluded under State rules. States operating Broad-Based Categorical Eligibility programs may exclude all resources from consideration. Liquid resources include cash on hand, money in checking or savings accounts, savings certificates, stocks or bonds, and lump-sum payments such as income tax refunds, retroactive lump-sum Social Security or SSI payments, and insurance settlements. The State agency shall verify all resources the household is questionable about or unclear whether to report. The equity value of resources, except for licensed vehicles, is the fair market value less encumbrances.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.7 (Work requirements)

Work-registered household members must comply with the work requirements of this section, which include registering for work, participating in work programs as assigned by the State agency, accepting suitable employment, and not voluntarily quitting a job or reducing work hours to less than thirty hours per week. Work registration is required for all household members between sixteen and fifty-nine years of age who are not exempt. Exempt individuals include persons employed at least thirty hours per week or earning weekly wages equal to the federal minimum wage multiplied by thirty hours; persons caring for a dependent child under six years of age or an incapacitated person; students enrolled at least half-time in school, training program, or institution of higher education; persons medically certified as physically or mentally unfit for employment; persons receiving unemployment compensation; and persons participating in drug addiction or alcohol treatment programs. Able-bodied adults without dependents between the ages of eighteen and fifty who do not meet specified work requirements are limited to three months of benefits in a thirty-six-month period unless they are employed or participating in a work program for at least twenty hours per week. The State agency shall determine whether an individual satisfies exemption criteria based on information provided on the application and through collateral contacts or other verification sources. Failure to comply with work requirements without good cause results in disqualification of the individual until compliance is achieved.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.9(d) (Deductions from income)

Households are entitled to deductions from income when calculating net income for benefit determination purposes. The standard deduction is a fixed amount based on household size that is adjusted annually for inflation. An earned income deduction of twenty percent of earned income is applied to recognize work expenses and taxes. Households with dependent care costs may deduct actual costs when necessary for a household member to work, seek employment, or attend training or education. Households with out-of-pocket medical expenses for elderly or disabled members may deduct the portion of expenses that exceeds thirty-five dollars per month for allowable medical costs including prescription drugs, medical supplies, health insurance premiums, and transportation to medical appointments. Households are entitled to a deduction for shelter costs that exceed fifty percent of the household's income after all other deductions. The shelter deduction is capped at a maximum amount that is adjusted annually, except for households with an elderly or disabled member, which receive an uncapped shelter deduction. Shelter costs include rent or mortgage payments, property taxes, insurance on the structure, required fees for condominium or cooperative housing, and a standard utility allowance or actual utility costs. Child support payments made to non-household members under a legal obligation are deducted from income. The State agency shall apply deductions in the order specified in this section when calculating net income: earned income deduction, dependent care deduction, medical expense deduction for elderly or disabled members, shelter cost deduction, and child support deduction.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.2(e) (Rights and responsibilities disclosure)

At the time of application and recertification, the State agency shall provide households with written notice of their rights and responsibilities under the program. The notice must inform households of their rights to apply for benefits, receive assistance on the same day they apply if they qualify for expedited service, be notified of their eligibility or ineligibility in a timely manner, receive an explanation of how benefits are calculated, receive a notice of adverse action before benefits are reduced or terminated, request a fair hearing, and obtain interpreter services at no cost. The notice must inform households of their responsibilities to provide accurate and complete information, report required changes during the certification period, participate in quality control reviews, cooperate with employment and training requirements, use benefits only to purchase eligible food items, and report the sale or trade of food benefits which is illegal. The State agency must inform households that intentional program violations including trafficking in benefits result in disqualification and prosecution, and that information provided on the application is subject to verification and computer matching. The household must acknowledge receipt of the rights and responsibilities statement by signing the application form. The State agency shall maintain copies of the rights and responsibilities notice in languages other than English as necessary to ensure households with limited English proficiency understand the program rules.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.2(b)(3) (Certification and signature)

Prior to certification, a household member or authorized representative must sign the application form under penalty of perjury attesting to the truth and completeness of the information provided. The signature certifies that the household understands the rights and responsibilities, will use the benefits to purchase eligible food for the household, and acknowledges that providing false information is subject to prosecution under State and Federal law. The signature also authorizes the State agency to verify information through collateral contacts, computer matches, and other means permitted by law and regulation. For electronically submitted applications, the State agency may accept an electronic signature that complies with State law and is attributable to the applicant. For applications submitted by mail, the signature must be an original handwritten signature unless State procedures permit alternative signature methods. The State agency shall not certify a household without an authorized signature on the application. If the applicant is unable to sign the application due to a disability, the State agency shall accept a signature by mark witnessed by a State agency representative or another individual. The authorized representative who signs on behalf of a household must provide documentation establishing their authority to act for the household, which may include a signed statement from the household, a power of attorney, or court documentation establishing guardianship or other legal authority.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.2(n) (Authorized representatives)

A household may designate an authorized representative to act on its behalf in applying for and using SNAP benefits. The authorized representative may be another person, an organization, or an institution acting with the household's written consent. The household must designate the authorized representative in writing on a form provided by the State agency or through other written documentation acceptable to the State agency. The authorized representative may complete the application, participate in the interview, provide verification documents, and receive and use the household's Electronic Benefits Transfer card to purchase food on behalf of the household. The State agency may not restrict a household's ability to designate an authorized representative except that the State agency may require that representatives for drug addict or alcoholic household members be designated by the State alcohol or drug agency, and may prohibit individuals disqualified for program violations from serving as authorized representatives. The authorized representative is responsible for complying with program rules and is subject to the same penalties as the household for program violations including misuse of benefits. The State agency shall provide notices and other communications to both the household and the authorized representative unless the household requests that communications be sent only to the authorized representative. The household may change or terminate the designation of an authorized representative at any time by providing written notice to the State agency. The State agency may terminate an authorized representative's authority if the representative fails to comply with program requirements or if the State agency determines through reliable information that the representative is not acting in the household's best interest.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.10(a) (Benefit determination — general)

The State agency shall calculate the household's monthly benefits based on the household's net income and household size using the Thrifty Food Plan as adjusted annually for inflation. The Thrifty Food Plan represents the cost of a nutritionally adequate diet prepared at home for a family of four and is adjusted for households of different sizes. For households with no net income, the benefit amount equals the maximum allotment for the household size. For households with net income, the benefit amount is calculated by multiplying net monthly income by thirty percent and subtracting the result from the maximum allotment. The minimum benefit for one and two person households is established annually by statute. Benefits are prorated for the month of application based on the date of application. For initial applications, benefits are prorated from the date of application to the end of the month. For applications denied and subsequently approved following a fair hearing, benefits are provided retroactive to the original application date. Benefits are issued monthly through Electronic Benefits Transfer cards that can be used at authorized retail food stores and farmers' markets to purchase eligible food items. The State agency shall ensure that benefits are available for use by the household no later than the issuance date established for the household based on case number, name, or other uniform criteria. Households have twelve months to use benefits before they expire and are removed from the account.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.12(a) (Reporting changes)

Households are required to report certain changes in circumstances during the certification period. The changes that must be reported and the timeframe for reporting depend on the household's reporting system. Households assigned to simplified reporting are required to report only changes in gross monthly income that exceed one hundred thirty percent of the poverty level for the household size and changes in household composition. Households assigned to change reporting are required to report all changes in household circumstances that affect eligibility or benefit level within ten days of the date the change becomes known to the household. Changes that must be reported include changes in income exceeding one hundred dollars per month; changes in household composition including addition or loss of a member; changes in residence and resulting changes in shelter costs; acquisition of a vehicle or other resource to the extent it affects eligibility; changes in legal obligation to pay child support; changes in the source of income; and for able-bodied adults without dependents, changes in work hours. The State agency shall establish procedures for households to report changes by telephone, in writing, in person, or electronically. The State agency shall act on reported changes by determining whether they affect eligibility or benefit level and shall issue a notice of adverse action if the change results in benefit reduction or termination. If the change results in an increase in benefits, the State agency shall issue the increased benefits no later than the month following the month the change was reported. The State agency shall not reduce or terminate benefits without providing advance notice except when the household has clearly expressed in writing that it no longer wishes to receive benefits or when the certification period has expired.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.2(f) (Verification requirements)

The State agency shall require verification of certain information as a condition of eligibility. Mandatory verification at initial application includes gross nonexempt income, alien status, utility expenses when the household elects to claim actual utility costs, medical expenses claimed as a deduction, Social Security numbers for all household members applying for benefits, residency in the project area, identity of the applicant or authorized representative, disability status when claimed for special deductions or exemptions, and for able-bodied adults without dependents, hours worked or participation in a qualifying work activity. Other factors of eligibility, such as household composition, shelter costs, and dependent care expenses, need not be verified unless questionable. Information is questionable if it is inconsistent with statements made by the applicant, inconsistent with other information on the application or previous applications, or inconsistent with information received from another source. Acceptable verification sources include documentary evidence such as wage stubs, award letters, utility bills, rent receipts, lease agreements, medical bills, tax records, or school records. When documentary evidence is unavailable, the State agency may accept collateral contacts from sources who can corroborate the household's statements such as employers, landlords, social service agencies, or neighbors. Home visits may be conducted only when documentary evidence is insufficient and the household agrees to the visit. The State agency shall not delay a determination of eligibility beyond thirty days solely because verification is pending; if the household has provided all verification within its power to obtain, the State agency shall assist the household in obtaining remaining verification. The State agency shall provide written notice to the household of all verification required, the deadlines for submission, and the consequences of failing to provide verification, which may include denial or delay of benefits.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.10(e) (Net income determination and eligibility tests)

Households that are not categorically eligible must satisfy both a gross income test and a net income test to qualify for SNAP benefits. Gross monthly income includes all earned and unearned income of household members before any deductions, excluding specifically excluded income such as certain educational income, income of ineligible household members not counted under regulation, and reimbursements for past or future expenses. Gross income must be at or below one hundred thirty percent of the federal poverty level for the household size. Households containing an elderly or disabled member are exempt from the gross income test and must satisfy only the net income test. Net monthly income is calculated by subtracting from gross income the standard deduction for the household size, an earned income deduction of twenty percent of gross earned income, the dependent care deduction, the medical expense deduction for elderly or disabled members, the excess shelter cost deduction up to the shelter cap, and any legally obligated child support paid to non-household members. Net income must be at or below one hundred percent of the federal poverty level for the household size. A household that fails either applicable income test is ineligible regardless of other circumstances. The State agency shall anticipate the household's income for the certification period based on income received during the application month and prior months, information about expected changes in income, and the household's stated understanding of future circumstances. When income fluctuates, the State agency may average income over a representative period. The federal poverty level amounts used in the income tests are adjusted annually and published by the Department of Health and Human Services.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.2(j) (Categorical eligibility)

Households in which all members receive or are authorized to receive cash assistance under Temporary Assistance for Needy Families, Supplemental Security Income, or State or local general assistance programs are categorically eligible for SNAP without a separate determination of resources or gross and net income. Broad-Based Categorical Eligibility allows States to confer categorical eligibility on households receiving non-cash benefits or services funded by TANF or maintenance-of-effort expenditures, such as informational brochures, referral services, or subsidized child care. When a State elects Broad-Based Categorical Eligibility, the State may raise the gross income limit up to two hundred percent of the federal poverty level and may eliminate the resource test for categorically eligible households. Categorically eligible households must still satisfy non-financial eligibility requirements including citizenship or qualified alien status, work requirements, Social Security number provision, and cooperation with child support enforcement when required. Categorically eligible households must still complete an application and participate in an interview. Benefit amounts for categorically eligible households are calculated based on the household's net income using the standard benefit calculation, except that categorically eligible households with no net income receive at least the minimum benefit. Categorical eligibility does not waive the requirement to verify factors that affect benefit amount such as income, shelter costs, and household size. A household loses categorical eligibility when it no longer receives or is authorized to receive the qualifying benefit, at which point the State agency shall redetermine eligibility under regular SNAP rules.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.5 (Student eligibility)

An individual enrolled at least half-time in an institution of higher education is ineligible for SNAP unless the individual satisfies one of several specific exemptions. Institutions of higher education include business, technical, trade, and vocational schools that normally require a high school diploma or equivalent for enrollment, as well as colleges and universities offering associate, bachelor, graduate, or professional degrees. Exemptions that preserve student eligibility include being under age eighteen or over age fifty; being physically or mentally unfit for employment; receiving TANF benefits; being employed at least twenty hours per week or self-employed earning weekly earnings equal to the federal minimum wage multiplied by twenty hours; participating in an on-the-job training program; being enrolled as a result of participation in a Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act program, an employment and training program under SNAP, or a similar program operated by a State or local government; being a single parent enrolled full-time in higher education and caring for a dependent child under age twelve; being a parent with a dependent child under age six or a dependent child between six and eleven for whom adequate child care is not available; being responsible for more than half the care of a dependent child under age twelve; or being assigned to or placed in an institution of higher education through a program specified in regulation. The State agency shall verify student status and any claimed exemption. A student who fails to maintain the qualifying exemption during a certification period becomes ineligible and the State agency shall take action to remove the student from the household for SNAP purposes. Students residing in institutional settings such as dormitories that provide the majority of meals are ineligible regardless of whether they satisfy an exemption.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.24 (Able-bodied adults without dependents time limit)

Able-bodied adults without dependents between eighteen and fifty years of age who are not pregnant and who are not responsible for a dependent child are subject to a time limit of three months of SNAP benefits in any thirty-six-month period unless they satisfy a work requirement. To maintain eligibility beyond three months, the individual must work at least twenty hours per week averaged over a month, participate in a qualifying work program at least twenty hours per week, combine work and qualifying work program participation for at least twenty hours per week, or participate in and comply with a workfare program. Exemptions from the time limit include medically certified physical or mental unfitness for employment, pregnancy, responsibility for a dependent child, exemption from the general work requirements under other regulatory provisions, residence in an area with an approved waiver based on high unemployment or insufficient jobs, and use of a discretionary exemption granted by the State agency. States may request waivers from the time limit for areas with unemployment rates over ten percent or with insufficient jobs. States may also grant individual fifteen percent exemptions to a limited number of able-bodied adults without dependents who would otherwise lose benefits. The State agency shall inform affected individuals of the time limit, the work requirement, available exemptions, and the consequences of failing to comply. The State agency shall track the three countable months in the thirty-six-month period for each affected individual. A countable month is a month in which the individual received SNAP benefits without satisfying the work requirement or qualifying for an exemption. Individuals who lose eligibility due to the time limit may regain eligibility by working or participating in a qualifying work program for at least eighty hours within a thirty-day period.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.10(f) (Certification periods)

The State agency shall certify eligible households for a definite certification period. Certification periods generally range from one to twelve months for most households and up to twenty-four months for households with elderly or disabled members whose income and circumstances are stable. The State agency shall establish certification periods consistent with the household's circumstances and the need to redetermine eligibility and benefit amounts. Households with unstable or fluctuating income may be assigned shorter certification periods to allow more frequent review. At the end of the certification period, the household must submit a recertification application to continue receiving benefits without interruption. Recertification requires a new application, a new interview, and verification of any changes in circumstances. The State agency shall provide notice to the household at least one month before the certification period ends informing the household of the need to apply for recertification, the procedures for doing so, and the consequences of failing to timely recertify. Households that submit a complete recertification application by the fifteenth day of the last month of the certification period are entitled to uninterrupted benefits if eligible. Households that fail to timely recertify lose benefits at the end of the certification period and must submit a new initial application to resume participation. The State agency may conduct a mid-certification review in longer certification periods to verify continued eligibility, during which the household is required to provide updated information and verification but is not required to complete a new application or interview.

## Section — 7 CFR 273.15 (Fair hearings)

Households have the right to a fair hearing to contest any action by the State agency that affects their participation in SNAP, including denial of benefits, reduction or termination of benefits, calculation of benefit amount, denial of expedited service, and any other adverse determination. The State agency must inform households of the right to a fair hearing at application, recertification, and any time an adverse action is taken. Requests for a fair hearing may be made orally or in writing and must be filed within ninety days of the action being contested. A household that requests a fair hearing within ten days of the notice of adverse action may continue to receive benefits at the previous level pending the outcome of the hearing, unless the household specifically waives continuation of benefits. The hearing must be conducted by an impartial official who was not involved in the initial decision. The household has the right to examine all documents and records used in the decision before and during the hearing, to present witnesses and evidence, to be represented by legal counsel or another representative, to cross-examine witnesses, and to submit written arguments. The hearing decision must be issued within sixty days of the hearing request and must be based solely on the evidence presented at the hearing. If the hearing decision is in favor of the household, the State agency shall promptly restore any benefits that were improperly denied or reduced, including retroactive benefits for the period of the improper action. If the hearing decision is against the household, any benefits continued during the hearing process may be subject to recoupment as an overpayment.

## Section — Wisconsin FoodShare (Wis. Stat. 49.79 and Wis. Admin. Code DHS 1507)

Wisconsin administers SNAP as the FoodShare Wisconsin program under the authority of Wisconsin Statute 49.79 and Wisconsin Administrative Code DHS 1507. The Wisconsin Department of Health Services is the single State agency responsible for program administration, while applications are processed and ongoing case management is performed by eleven regional Income Maintenance consortia that serve all seventy-two Wisconsin counties. Applicants may apply online through the ACCESS portal at access.wisconsin.gov, by submitting a paper application to the local Income Maintenance agency, by telephone, or in person at a consortium office. The ACCESS portal provides a combined application for FoodShare and other Wisconsin public assistance programs including BadgerCare Plus health coverage, Wisconsin Shares child care subsidy, and Wisconsin Home Energy Assistance. Wisconsin issues FoodShare benefits through the QUEST card, an Electronic Benefits Transfer card that can be used at authorized retailers throughout the state and nationwide. The State agency must conduct an eligibility interview with a household member or authorized representative before approving benefits; interviews are typically conducted by telephone unless the applicant requests an in-person interview or the State agency determines an in-person interview is necessary. Wisconsin operates Broad-Based Categorical Eligibility for most households, which eliminates the resource test and raises the gross income limit to two hundred percent of the federal poverty level for non-elderly, non-disabled households. Households with an elderly or disabled member and households receiving Wisconsin Works cash benefits remain subject to specific categorical eligibility rules established under federal regulation. Wisconsin's FoodShare Employment and Training program, known as FSET, provides employment services to able-bodied adults without dependents and others subject to work requirements. Wisconsin has historically sought federal waivers from the ABAWD time limit in areas with sustained high unemployment, and the geographic scope of any active waiver is published annually by the Department of Health Services.
